The Well-Architected Review is the most underutilized consulting tool in Saudi Arabia's cloud market. Here is exactly how to run one in 5 days — and why every cloud engagement should begin with it. In 20+ years of IT infrastructure work, I have not found a single consulting engagement that generates more immediate, demonstrable client value than a Well-Architected Review. Here is why — and how to run one properly. What a WAR actually is: A structured AWS-endorsed assessment of a cloud workload against 6 pillars: Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance, Cost, and Sustainability. The AWS Well-Architected Tool (free, in the AWS console) walks you through 300+ questions and auto-generates the findings report. What most consultants get wrong: They present the raw AWS report. A PDF with 40 technical findings means nothing to a CIO who needs to justify a board-level decision. The consultant's job is to transform technical findings into business narrative. The 5-step process I use: Day 1 (AM): Scope the workload. Define the boundaries. Confirm stakeholders. Set up the tool. Days 2–4: Run 90-minute workshops for each pillar with the client's technical team. Document evidence. Assign risk levels. Do not allow solution design discussions — that comes later. Day 4 (PM): Analyze findings. Build the impact × effort prioritization matrix. Identify top 5 HRIs and top 3 quick wins. Day 5: Deliver the executive presentation. Lead with the risk exposure in SAR. Show the radar chart. Present the 90-day improvement roadmap. Close with a clear investment ask. The output is not a technical report. It is a business case for the next 12 months of advisory work.
