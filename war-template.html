Overview · Foundation
What is a Well-Architected Review?
A Well-Architected Review (WAR) is AWS's structured framework for evaluating cloud workloads against industry best practices. It is the single most powerful consulting engagement available to an AWS cloud advisor — generating immediate, demonstrable client value while creating a roadmap for ongoing advisory work.
🏛️ The AWS Well-Architected Framework was developed by AWS Solutions Architects to help customers build secure, high-performing, resilient, and efficient infrastructure. It defines 6 pillars, 58 design principles, and 300+ review questions.
⚡ Why Every Engagement Should Begin With a WAR

A Well-Architected Review performs three consulting functions simultaneously: it establishes your credibility as an AWS expert, creates a comprehensive current-state assessment the client can reference independently, and generates a prioritized improvement backlog that translates directly into subsequent advisory engagements. No other consulting tool delivers this return in a comparable time investment.

Overview · Methodology
WAR Engagement Process — Step by Step
A standard Well-Architected Review engagement runs 3–5 consultant days. This process applies whether you are conducting a review for a Saudi university, government ministry, or enterprise client.
1
Pre-Engagement Scoping (Half Day)
Define the workload under review — a specific application, platform, or business unit. Confirm the workload owner, technical lead, and business sponsor. Clarify objectives: are you looking for security posture, cost baseline, or full 6-pillar assessment? Document scope boundaries explicitly.
Deliverable: Scoping Document
2
AWS Well-Architected Tool Setup (1 Hour)
Log into AWS Console → Well-Architected Tool → Define Workload. Enter workload name, AWS regions, environment type, and team contacts. This tool provides the complete question set and auto-generates the findings report. Ensure you have read-only access to the client's AWS account for evidence gathering.
Tool: console.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected
3
Pillar Workshops — 90 Minutes Each (3 Days)
Run structured workshops for each pillar with the client's technical team. For each question: document current state, identify evidence, assign risk level (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / NONE). Keep workshops focused — do not allow scope creep into solution design. Record all responses in the AWS Well-Architected Tool.
6 workshops × 90 min = 9 hours facilitation
4
Findings Analysis & Prioritization (Half Day)
Export the AWS Well-Architected Tool report. Categorize all findings as High Risk Items (HRIs) or Medium Risk Items (MRIs). Build an Impact × Effort prioritization matrix. Identify the top 5 HRIs that pose the greatest business risk and the top 3 quick wins achievable within 30 days.
Output: Prioritized Risk Register
5
Executive Findings Presentation (Half Day)
Transform the technical findings into a business narrative. Lead with the headline risk exposure, not the technical detail. Present the radar chart, heat map, and improvement roadmap. Close with a clear investment summary and recommended next steps. Never present the raw AWS report — translate it into client language.
Deliverable: Executive Briefing (this template)

Overview · Preparation
Pre-Engagement Checklist
Complete every item before your first client workshop. Missing preparation items cause delays mid-engagement and reduce consultant credibility.
  • Client scoping document signed and workload boundaries agreed
  • AWS Well-Architected Tool workload created in client's AWS account
  • Read-only AWS account access obtained for evidence gathering
  • Workload owner, technical lead, and business sponsor identified
  • All 6 pillar workshop sessions scheduled (90 min each)
  • Architecture diagrams obtained from client team
  • Current incident/outage history reviewed (last 12 months)
  • NCA / PDPL / regulatory scope documented (Saudi clients)
  • Executive presentation slot confirmed post-review (within 1 week)
  • Report template customized with client logo, colors, and workload name

Roadmap · Improvement Plan
Prioritized Improvement Roadmap
Structure all WAR improvement items into three phases: 90-day quick wins (high impact, low effort), 6-month core improvements (architectural changes), and 12-month strategic initiatives (governance and optimization maturity).
✅ Consulting Tip: Never present more than 5 items per phase to the executive team. Group smaller items and lead with the one that creates the most visible business value. Each phase should have a named owner and a clear success metric.
🚨 Phase 1 — Immediate Action (Days 1–90)

High-impact, low-effort items that eliminate the most critical business risk. These require no architectural redesign — they are configuration and policy changes.
